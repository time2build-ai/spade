Hands-on · ~10 min · no terminal

Build a todo app —
just by talking to Spade.

You’ll create a real todo app (a React front, a small API behind it) without writing a line of setup. You describe what you’re building; Spade plans it, fills the backlog, and puts agents on the work. You watch it happen.

①
Before you start: have Spade open in your browser, signed in, with at least one account connected (your teammate or whoever set up Spade will have done this — you’ll see accounts on the Agent pool page). That’s the only setup; everything else is in the app.
Step 1

Create your project

A project holds everything — its plan, backlog and agents.

🖱️
Top-left of the sidebar, click the project switcher → New project.
✍️
Name it Todo App and point it at a folder (e.g. a fresh ~/code/todo). Create.
Open Backlog — four empty columns. Your blank canvas.
Step 2 · the main event

Just say what you’re building

Don’t list features or decisions — describe the app like you’d tell a teammate. Spade does the extracting.

⌘
Press ⌘K to open Ask the brain, and type it out:
💬 You say
“Hey, let’s start a little todo app. People jot down todos, check them off when they’re done, edit or delete them, and filter to just the open ones — and down the line, due dates would be nice. I’m picturing a React + Vite front end with a small FastAPI + SQLite back end. Can you set up the brain and a starter backlog?”

From those few sentences — no “create an ADR”, no lists — Spade pulls out and proposes:

  • Decisions — React + Vite (front) and FastAPI + SQLite (back), recorded as proposed for you to confirm.
  • Features — add, complete, edit / delete, filter, due dates, storage.
  • A starter backlog — back-end and front-end tasks, each grounded in its feature.

It replies with what it captured — e.g. “Captured 2 decisions, 6 features and 10 tasks. Want me to mark the stack decisions active?”

That’s the whole idea. You talk about the product; Spade keeps the plan and backlog honest. You never fill in forms.
Step 3

See what it understood

Open these and check it heard you right.

Decisions — the stack choices it pulled from your paragraph, each a real ADR.
Product brain — every feature it heard, as a connected node. Graph & Issues already flags any feature with no decision yet.
Step 4

Your backlog — already drafted

Back-end and front-end work, as cards, each tied to its feature.

🖱️
Open Backlog, then click any card to see its detail and the feature it’s grounded in.
Cards ready to go, across the columns. (shown with a few already moved along)
Step 5 · backlog from a meeting

A meeting becomes backlog

Backlog doesn’t only come from chat. Spade can ingest a meeting and turn its action items into grounded cards — the same way a real notes tool (Granola, Otter, Fireflies) would feed it.

🖱️
Open Meetings → Ingest a meeting, and pick the Todo App — kickoff transcript from a connected source.
The meeting lands with its summary and transcript — and every action line (the → ones) becomes a backlog card, each grounded back to what was said in the room. They show up on the Backlog board too.
For the demo the transcripts are canned (no tokens, always the same) — swapping in a live notes integration is just pointing ingest at real text.
Step 6

Put an agent on a task live agent

Pick where to start — just say so. Spade runs that task through its pipeline (developer → reviewer → integrator → documentor).

💬 You say
“Let’s get started on the create-todo endpoint — put a developer on it.”
Open Orchestrator — your run, moving through its stages. Sprints updates too, straight from the real runs.
Step 7

What Spade noticed on its own

It reasons over your graph and surfaces what’s missing.

🖱️
Open Graph & Issues.
It flags an undecided feature and any unresolved proposal. Mention the missing decision in chat and watch the flag clear.
That’s the loop

Keep talking — it keeps up

From here you just work out loud. Spade captures as you go.

💬 You say
“I’m thinking we add dark mode — probably theme it with CSS variables. Users have been asking for it, too.”

You proposed nothing explicitly — but Spade hears a feature (dark mode), a decision (CSS-variable theming, as proposed) and feedback (users asking), and offers to capture all three. Watch the brain and backlog update.
